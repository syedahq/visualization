GOOD VISUALIZATION: 

     Link: https://ig.ft.com/coronavirus-chart/?areas=eur&areas=usa&areas=jpn&areas=grc&areas=nzl&areas=e92000001&areasRegional=usny&areasRegional=usnm&areasRegional=uspr&areasRegional=usaz&areasRegional=usfl&areasRegional=usnd&cumulative=0&logScale=0&per100K=1&startDate=2022-01-01&values=deaths

     The visualization that performs a comparison of COVID19 death rates across different countries, clearly communicates a complex set of information that maintains accuracy and critically, it reduces unwarranted cognitive load. As learned in class, to help the audience understand trends in a more straightforward and intuitive way, it is important to select the appropriate type of representation for the data being presented. In this case, a line graphs was an effective selection because it can show how changes in COVID19 death rates occur over time. Also, the line graphs show strong perceptual qualities. The purpose of the line graph is to compare mortality trends across different countries throughout 2022. Essentially, we can see that countries are labelled at the end of each line, preventing viewers from having to constantly refer to a legend to understand the plot. Therefore, the viewers can focus on the data represented and overall, this graph type reduces cognitive load. In addition, we learned that Kennedy et al. (2016) demonstrated that two-dimensional layouts, clean designs, geometric lines and visible data sources enhance perceptions of objectivity and trustworthiness. Keeping this in mind, this visualization is in alignment with several of these conventions which contributed to increased perceived factual basis of this graphic. Also, the source information is clearly displayed at the bottom of the visualizations demonstrating provenance rhetoric and increasing transparency. Lastly, the chart shows data standardized per 100k people instead of raw counts. This enables meaningful comparisons across different population sizes and enhances the substantiative quality of the visualization by accurately representing the underlying data. 
     In terms of improvement, I would suggest that line colors could be difficult for individuals with color vision deficiencies to distinguish. A more accessible color selection would enhance inclusivity and accessibility. In addition, I would say that the graph is to an extent crowded when multiple countries converge towards the end of the year. Interaction filters or small multiple charts could help decrease the clutter from a visual standpoint and further decrease cognitive load while preserving the same information. 
     

BAD VISUALIZATION: 
     
     Link: https://flowingdata.com/2011/12/12/fox-news-still-makes-awesome-charts/ 

     I would select the fox news unemployment rate chart as a bad visualization due to its choice for designs, the potential for a misleading impression of the magnitude of change in the underlying data. 

     Firstly, the issue exists with the truncated y-axis, the axis starts at around 8% rather than 0% causing relatively minute changes in unemployment to appear larger than they are in reality. In addition, although the reported values themselves are correct, the visual representation accentuates the differences between months. This shows how a visualization can be correct without being neutral which is a concept we discussed in the class and in D’Ignazio and Klein (2020) discussion of data visualization as a rhetorical object. Secondly, the visualization employs a large shaded red area below the line, viewers often interpret area as representation for magnitude. The dramatic red shading amplifies the idea of growth in unemployed and promotes a stronger emotional response form the viewer than would be the case had this been a simpler line chart. Also, this increases the rhetorical effect of the visualization rather than helping viewers objectively interpret the data. Thirdly, by directing attention towards a specific narrative through the title, the visualization weakens it perceptual quality. By including President Obama’s name in the title alongside exaggerated visual scaling, it propels viewers to attribute changes in unemployment to a political figure. Although, I understand that data visualization is not completely neutral, this selection for design priorities persuasion over clear communication. Lastly, the chart does not follow the conventions linked with perceived objectivity. Relative to the examples learned in class, the design is visually dramatic and uses style elements that increase the extraneous cognitive load. Thus, it forces viewers to work harder to distinguish the actual numerical changes from the visual emphasis. 
     This could be improved by ensuring that the y-axis starts at zero or clearly relay that an axis break is being used. This would enable a more proportional representation of changes in unemployment and reduce the risk of misleading the audience. Also, removing the shaded red area and using a simple two-dimensional line graphs would allow a better design that aligns with conventions linked with perceived factuality and would decrease unnecessary visual emphasis. Lastly, it would be nice if more context was provided like unemployment trends over a greater period, this would help viewers interpret data more accurately instead of focusing a subset of values.
